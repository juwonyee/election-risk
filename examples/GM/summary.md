**Locked in regardless:**
- P.L. 119-21: consumer EV credits (30D/45W) ended 2025-09-30, CAFE civil penalties set to $0, 45X phase-down; repeal needs the President's signature through Jan 2029
- Section 232 auto (15-25%) and metals tariffs and Section 301 tariffs in force; GM guides -$2.5B to -$3.5B gross tariff cost for 2026; Congress has no disapproval route
- UAW contract runs to 2028

**Weakest links:**
- USMCA withdrawal impact: 0.69M GM vehicles from Mexico/Canada (Inovev, 2024) x ~$40,000 customs value (estimated) x 25% tariff x ~25% US content that becomes dutiable (estimated) = ~$1.7B; parts exposure not sized. Both the $40,000 and the 25% US-content share are my estimates.
- KXUSMCA-26 resolves on a withdrawal NOTICE; withdrawal takes effect 6 months later and a notice can be a bargaining step, so the impact assumes the notice leads to loss of USMCA treatment (rules only partly match).
- Tariff-repeal impact uses GM's 2026 gross tariff guide midpoint ($3.0B) as if all of it went away; part is metals and Korea tariffs that a narrower law might leave in place.
- Book row doubt (US-house-control.md): the Republican tariffs-trade row ('block: veto', p 0.07) prices keeping the tariffs, not repealing them; I set Republicans' p for a repeal law to 0 because no Republican row proposes it.
- Book row doubt (US-house-control.md): Democrats 'bring tariff-disapproval resolutions to the floor' is 'make: alone' p 0.45, but Section 232/301 tariffs have no disapproval route (policy.md), so it cannot move GM's tariff bill; not used.
- Book row doubt (MI-governor.md): Benson 'subnational agreements with Canada' is 'make: alone' p 0.45 although the row itself says its effect on federal tariffs is unclear; a governor has no tariff lever, so it is not linked.
