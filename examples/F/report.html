Red Team

Weak spots a skeptical reviewer would raise.
  • USMCA withdrawal size (~$2.0B a year) is an estimate: Hermosillo volume annualised from Jan-Jul 2025 US sales, an assumed ~$25k import value and ~50% of value not yet tariffed; the parts share leans on a Reuters-sourced report (via Motor16) that tighter content rules alone cost Ford >= $2B a year. Ford's mitigation (pricing, sourcing shifts) is not netted
  • KXUSMCA-26-27 resolves on a withdrawal NOTICE before 2027; withdrawal takes effect 6 months later and a notice can be a negotiating move that is later withdrawn. The same notice trades at 0.175 in KXUSMCAOUTCOMES-27-USW (2.4k contracts) vs 0.085 here (15k contracts): two Kalshi prices for one event
  • Book row doubted: US-house-control.md gives a tariff repeal becoming law p 0.07 under a Republican House (block: veto) but only 0.05 under a Democratic House (federal: partisan). A Democratic House should not make repeal less likely; the swing sign is likely an artefact of the probability-table cells, not of politics
  • Book row doubted: CA-governor.md has no clean-cars-ev row for Steve Hilton, though another book file notes both candidates oppose the 2035 gas-car ban; Hilton is therefore 0 here
  • Book row doubted: MI-governor.md gives Benson's 'subnational agreements with Canada' p 0.45 (make: alone) although a governor cannot change federal tariffs; not used here
  • Compliance-credit sizing for California (~$100M) assumes ~15% of Ford's ~$700M credit purchases relate to California/ZEV-state rules; Ford does not split them, and California's ACC II waiver was revoked by Congress in 2025 (in litigation), so the line may already be near zero
  • Tariff-repeal impact (+$1.0B) assumes a repeal law would reach Section 232 auto and parts tariffs; Democratic proposals are not specific
