Red Team

Weak spots a skeptical reviewer would raise.
  • PRO Act impact (-$120M) assumes union coverage reaching ~20% of US store labor at a ~8% union premium; Starbucks discloses only that ~6% of company-operated stores are unionized (estimated)
  • Federal $17 by 2030 impact (-$100M) assumes ~30% of barista hours still below $17 in 2030 at a ~$1 average gap, doubled for wage compression; Starbucks does not disclose its pay distribution (estimated, from job-posting and pay-survey averages)
  • NYC $30 impact uses ~250 NYC stores (Manhattan 160+ after the Sep 2025 closures, plus the outer boroughs: estimated) and the national average of ~27,700 store labor hours per store
  • NYC cannot set its own minimum wage under state preemption; a $30 NYC wage would need Albany, and the policy book has no Hochul or NY-legislature row on it, so the Nov 3 New York races cannot be tied to this market (book gap)
  • Book row US-senate-control 'Republicans: keep confirming Trump labor nominees' (appoint, 0.70) prices a confirmation, not a labor-cost policy; the Board majority is already seated, so it is left out here rather than used as a race link
  • Wisconsin: Crowley's row bundles '$15 now' (no effect on Starbucks) with '$20 by 2030' (the part that matters); Tiffany's block-veto 0.07 applies to the $20 row
  • New York City enacts a $30 minimum wage by 2030: the impact is an estimate, not a reported number.
