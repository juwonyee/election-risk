# Starbucks (SBUX): 2026 election exposure
Base: FY2025 (ended 2025-09-28) revenue $37,184.4M, operating income $2,936.6M (*verified*, [EDGAR 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/829224/000082922425000114/0000829224-25-000114-index.htm)). FY25 OI is depressed by $892.0M of restructuring and impairments (~240 bp of margin); FY26 guidance is >11% consolidated operating margin ([Q3 FY26 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000829224/000082922426000129/sbux-06282026xearningsrele.htm)). Percentages below use FY25 OI.
Key concentration facts (*verified*, [FY25 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/829224/000082922425000114/sbux-20250928.htm)):
- North America is 74% of revenue ($27,373.1M). North America store operating expenses were $13,973.3M and North America product and distribution costs were $7,628.7M.
- There are 10,047 US company-operated stores and about 223,000 US employees (381,000 worldwide). Because Starbucks runs its US stores itself, wages hit its P&L directly.
- Partners in about 6% of US company-operated stores are union-represented, and there is no first contract.
- Green coffee is 10-15% of product and distribution costs, per the CFO ([Nasdaq/Zacks](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/starbucks-faces-looming-brazil-coffee-tariffs-can-it-absorb-hit)). That works out to $11,658M x 10-15% = **$1.17-1.75B/yr** worldwide (*estimated*).
- The FY25 10-K does not split US stores by state. Third-party counts of *all* locations (company-operated plus licensed) show CA at about 3,056-3,219, NY at about 610, WA at about 741 and PA at about 473 ([xMap](https://www.xmap.ai/blog/how-many-starbucks-are-in-the-united-states)).

## Bottom line
- **In play (expected): about $12M/yr (about 0.4% of FY25 operating income). If everything happens: about $93M/yr (about 3.2%), with an upper case of about $180M (about 6%) if a forced first union contract spreads to the whole US workforce.**
- **Locked in regardless:**
  - Tariffs:
    - Brazil 25% Section 301 and the 10-12.5% Section 301 tariffs on 60 economies both **exempt coffee**.
    - The IEEPA tariff refunds already received "largely offset" the tariffs Starbucks paid in Q1-Q3 FY26. By an *estimated* margin bridge that is ≤$133M, booked once.
  - Wages already set by law: CA $16.90 statewide and $20 fast-food; NYC $17; WA $17.13; Seattle $21.30.
  - Federal law: the OBBBA SNAP cuts and the no-tax-on-tips deduction.
- **Card fact:** Coffee beans are exempt from Trump's new Brazil tariff, so Starbucks' real election risk is barista pay in New York City and California, worth less than half a percent of profit on expected odds.
- **Strongest R-vs-D contrast:** the NY governor race. A Hochul (D, 94.5%) win keeps open a path for Albany to let NYC phase in a $30 wage, worth about $52M/yr to Starbucks by 2030. A Blakeman (R) win effectively closes it. The contrast is muted because the market already prices Hochul near certain and the enactment odds are low even under a D governor. Federal races barely matter for Starbucks' tariffs: Congress has no disapproval route for Section 301, and coffee is already exempt.

### 1. New York City $30 minimum wage
- **Election and odds:** GOVPARTYNY-26: Hochul (D) bid 0.939 / ask 0.940, mid **94.5%**, vol 541,714; Blakeman (R) mid 6.05%, vol 2,037,540. KXNYCMINWAGE-27JAN01 ("bill establishing ≥$30 for NYC effective 2030 or earlier becomes law before Jan 1, 2027"): bid 0.05 / ask 0.095, mid **7.25%**, vol 20,409. State legislature control (both chambers D) also matters but has no market.
- **Policy:** Council Intro 757, introduced 2026-03-10 by CM Sandy Nurse, is in committee. It sets $30 by 2030 for employers with more than 500 employees ([intro.nyc](https://intro.nyc/0757-2026+), [ABC7](https://abc7ny.com/post/nyc-council-considers-bill-raise-citys-minimum-wage-nation-leading-30-hour/18699477/)). Mamdani's schedule is $20 (2027), $23.50 (2028), $27 (2029), $30 (2030), then indexed ([City & State](https://www.cityandstateny.com/policy/2025/02/mamdani-unveils-30-30-minimum-wage-push-part-mayoral-campaign/403015/)). Under current law the state legislature sets the NYC wage, so the city needs Albany to authorize it. NYC's minimum wage is $17 in 2026 ([BK Mag](https://www.bkmag.com/2026/01/01/nyc-minimum-wage-increase-2026/)).
- **Enactment:** *Estimated* 15% by 2030 under a D governor. The market prices only 7% for a city law by year-end, Hochul has not publicly endorsed the plan, and a home-rule grant has to pass the legislature. Under an R governor it is *estimated* at 2%, because a veto is expected.
- **Size and exposure:**
  - NYC company-operated stores: about 280 (*estimated*). About 160 are in Manhattan after 25 closures there ([West Side Spirit](https://www.westsidespirit.com/home/starbucks-closing-25-manhattan-stores-six-on-uws-XA5130880)); 42 NYC closures since Dec 2024 ([Commercial Observer](https://commercialobserver.com/2025/12/starbucks-nyc-closures-chain-stores-report/)). NYC stores are essentially all company-operated.
  - About 20 hourly partners per store (*estimated*: 223,000 US employees / 10,047 stores ≈ 22, less support staff).
  - About 1,144 hours per year each (*estimated*: 22 h/wk).
  - NYC average barista pay is about $22.50/h (*estimated*, [Gridwise](https://gridwise.io/blog/starbucks-pay)).
- **Dollars:** `280 stores x 20 partners x 1,144 h x ($30.00 - $22.50) x 1.08 payroll tax = $51.9M/yr` at 2030 run-rate. The 2027 step to $20 costs about $0 because it is below current pay. **Expected:** `$51.9M x (0.945 x 0.15 + 0.055 x 0.02) = $7.4M`.

### 2. California Fast Food Council
- **Election and odds:** GOVPARTYCA-26: Becerra (D) bid 0.961 / ask 0.965, mid **96.3%**, vol 487,069; Hilton (R) mid 3.8%, vol 1,402,961. The governor appoints council members.
- **Policy:** Under AB 1228 the fast-food minimum wage is $20 (since 2024-04-01), and the council may raise it each year by min(3.5%, CPI-W). A proposed $20.70 step was never voted. The council "lacks a leader, hasn't met in over a year" ([CalMatters, May 2026](https://calmatters.org/economy/2026/05/fast-food-council-no-chairperson/); [Fisher Phillips](https://www.fisherphillips.com/en/insights/insights/californias-fast-food-council-plans)). Neither candidate has a stated position on the council (not found). Becerra's platform favors using "the power of the state to lower prices" and Hilton's favors lower taxes ([Daily Signal](https://dailysignal.com/2026/10/13/affordability-deciding-factor-californias-governor-race)). Starbucks is a covered limited-service chain under AB 1228.
- **Enactment:** *Estimated* 40% under a D governor (a new governor would fill the council, but the council has been dormant under a D governor). *Estimated* 5% under an R governor.
- **Size:** A Berkeley study of the $20 step found pay up about 11% and prices up about 1.5% (cited in [CalMatters](https://calmatters.org/economy/2026/05/fast-food-council-no-chairperson/)).
- **Exposure:** About 1,900 CA company-operated stores (*estimated*: about 3,100 total CA locations x 60% US company-operated share, 10,047/16,933). CA average pay is about $21/h ([Gridwise](https://gridwise.io/blog/starbucks-pay)). About 40% of partners sit near the floor (*estimated*), with an average lift of $0.50 including compression (*estimated*).
- **Dollars:** `1,900 x 20 x 1,144 h x 40% x $0.50 x 1.08 = $9.4M per annual step` (two steps by 2028 ≈ $19M). **Expected:** `$9.4M x (0.963 x 0.40 + 0.037 x 0.05) = $3.6M`.
- **Related local measures:** The Alameda County $30 measure was **pulled from the Nov 2026 ballot** by a 3-2 supervisors' vote on 2026-08-04, so it now goes to 2028 at the earliest. It would also cover only unincorporated areas ([Piedmont Exedra](https://piedmontexedra.com/2026/08/alameda-county-supervisors-postpone-30-minimum-wage-measure-until-at-least-2028)). No CA statewide wage measure is on the Nov 2026 ballot (KXCABALLOTMEASURES-26NOV03 lists Props 1-5 and 37-45; none was found to be a wage measure; all have thin volume).

### 3. Union / NLRB: Faster Labor Contracts Act
- **Election and odds:** Senate D ~62%, House D ~90% (Kalshi, per brief). No bill-specific market was found.
- **Policy:** S.844 (Hawley) / H.R.5408 requires bargaining within 10 days of certification, mediation after 90 days, then binding arbitration of a first contract. The House version passed with 20 Republicans voting yes ([CHRO](https://www.chro.org/w/house-passes-faster-labor-contracts-act-more-pro-labor-legislation-on-the-horizon-)). In the Senate the R leadership has not scheduled it; it has 2 R and 14 D cosponsors and needs 60 votes ([Bloomberg Law, Aug 2026](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/us-law-week/bipartisan-bill-to-accelerate-labor-contracts-roils-employers); [Merkley](https://www.merkley.senate.gov/es/new-hawley-merkley-legislation-to-speed-up-labor-contracts-earns-teamsters-endorsement-bipartisan-support)). A D Senate would also control NLRB confirmations, but the effect of that on Starbucks is ambiguous and not sized.
- **Enactment:** *Estimated* 15% under a D Senate (a floor vote becomes likely, but it still needs about 13 R votes and the President's signature, and his position was not found). *Estimated* 5% under an R Senate.
- **Exposure:**
  - Unionized partners are in about 6% of stores (10-K). Union reporting says more than 14,000 baristas are unionized, there is no first contract, and the union went on a 131-day strike from Nov 2025 ([Atlanta Civic Circle](https://atlantaciviccircle.org/2026/03/25/barista-strike-starbucks-resume-bargaining-sbwu-union/)).
  - The union's March 2026 offer was a $17 floor, 4% annual raises and minimum staffing of 3 ([Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/daily-labor-report/starbucks-union-working-to-revive-long-stalled-contract-talks-1)).
  - The company's typical recent raise is about 2% ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/12/12/starbucks-gives-baristas-smaller-pay-raises-after-tough-year)).
  - Average hourly pay is more than $18, with a range of $15.25-$26 ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/12/12/starbucks-gives-baristas-smaller-pay-raises-after-tough-year)).
- **Dollars:**
  - Direct cost: `14,000 x 1,144 h x $19 x 2 pp extra raise = $6.1M/yr` (staffing floor not sized).
  - Upper case if terms are extended to all US hourly partners: `~200,000 x 1,144 h x $18.5 x 2% x ~1.0 = ~$85-90M` (*estimated*).
  - **Expected:** `$6.1M x (0.62 x 0.15 + 0.38 x 0.05) = $0.7M`.

### 4. Tariffs (Brazil and coffee origins)
- **Odds:** KXTARIFFRATEBR-27JAN01, Brazil general rate on Jan 1, 2027. All rungs are **thin**.

  | Rung | Bid / ask | Mid | Volume |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | ≥10% | 0.76 / 0.84 | 80% | 2 |
  | ≥20% | 0.38 / 0.47 | 42.5% | 0 |
  | ≥30% | 0.15 / 0.24 | 19.5% | 66 |
  | ≥50% | 0.03 / 0.12 | 7.5% | 0 |

  The current rate is 25%, so the market leans toward a cut, but this is executive discretion, not an election outcome. No Kalshi markets for Colombia or Vietnam tariff rates were found.
- **Policy (coffee is exempt):**
  - USTR's Brazil Section 301 final action (2026-07-15, effective 2026-07-22) is a 25% negative list. Exemptions are "led by crude oil and coffee" and cover $20.1bn of 2024 imports; unflavored instant coffee was added in the final order ([Global Trade Alert](https://globaltradealert.org/blog/brazil-section-301-final-action); [USTR FRN](https://ustr.gov/sites/default/files/files/Issue_Areas/Enforcement/Section%20301/Brazil%20301%20Final%20Action%20FRN%207-15-2026%20final.pdf); [Cultivar](https://revistacultivar.com/news/US-exempts-Brazilian-coffee-from-an-additional-%2425-tariff.)).
  - The 60-economy forced-labor Section 301 tariffs (10%/12.5%, effective 2026-07-24) have a general exemption for products that cannot be grown in sufficient quantity in the US, "for example, coffee". Colombia and Vietnam are in the 12.5% tier ([Federal Register 2026-15181](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/07/28/2026-15181/notice-of-actions-in-section-301-investigations-of-acts-policies-and-practices-of-various-economies); [Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/07/and-the-tariff-beat-goes-on)).
  - Green coffee is MFN duty-free.
  - I did not check line-by-line HTS 0901 coverage against the annex. That remains a residual risk.
- **Congress:**
  - The Senate passed Kaine's Brazil IEEPA-emergency resolution 52-48 on 2025-10-28 with 5 Republicans ([PBS](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/senate-votes-to-block-tariffs-on-brazil-it-shows-some-pushback-to-trump-trade-policy)). The House blocked it, and a veto was expected.
  - That route no longer applies: IEEPA tariffs were struck down, and Section 301 has no disapproval mechanism. A new Congress would need a veto-proof law. P is about 2% (*estimated*, given a peak GOP crossover of 5 senators and 6 House members).
- **Exposure:**
  - Starbucks said tariffs and coffee drove about 120 bp of P&D inflation in H1 FY26 ([10-Q Q2 FY26](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000829224/000082922426000080/sbux-20260329.htm)).
  - The Q3 FY26 IEEPA refunds "largely offset" FY26 tariffs. North America non-GAAP margin rose about 280 bp, or more than 100 bp excluding refunds ([Q3 FY26 call](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/08/07/starbucks-sbux-q3-2026-earnings-call-transcript/)). The implied refund is ≤180 bp x $7,395M = **≤$133M** (*estimated*).
  - The remaining Section 301 tariffs fall on non-coffee goods (merchandise, equipment, packaging). The amount is not disclosed (not found).
- **Dollars:** If Congress repealed the Section 301 tariffs on Starbucks' non-coffee imports, the benefit would be **+$10-40M**, midpoint $25M (*estimated*: a fraction of the IEEPA-era run-rate, at lower 10-25% rates). **Expected:** `$25M x 0.02 = $0.5M`.
- **Not election-driven, for scale:** if the President removed the coffee exemption, the cost would be `$1.46B green coffee x ~60% US x ~25% Brazil share x 25% = ~$55M/yr` (*estimated*; Starbucks' origin mix is not disclosed).

### 5. PA, WI, GA (and WA) state minimum wages
- **Odds:** GOVPARTYPA-26 D 98.0% (vol 205K/492K); GOVPARTYWI-26 D 80.5% (vol 524K/825K); GOVPARTYGA-26 D 45.5% / R 53.5% (vol 635K/741K).
- **Why the direct effect is about $0:** All three states use the $7.25 federal floor. D proposals cluster around $15, which is below Starbucks' $15.25 US pay floor ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/12/12/starbucks-gives-baristas-smaller-pay-raises-after-tough-year)). The only effect would be indirect, through competitors' wages, and is not sized.
- **Washington:** WA has no governor race in 2026. Its $17.13 state wage and Seattle's $21.30 are CPI-indexed under existing law, so they are locked ([WA State Standard](https://washingtonstatestandard.com/briefs/washingtons-minimum-wage-to-top-17-in-2026/)).
- **Market check:** KXMINWAGECOUNT-27DEC31 (states enacting increases in 2027) shows ≥3 states at about 42.5% but zero volume, so it is not used.

### 6. SNAP and the consumer
SNAP cannot buy hot prepared food, so Starbucks is not a SNAP retailer. A Dec 12 lapse (Kalshi ~33%) would affect it only through broad low-income spending. Starbucks did not mention SNAP on its Q3 FY26 call. The impact is treated as about $0 and not quantified.

## Locked in regardless
- **Tariffs:**
  - Brazil 25% Section 301 and the 10-12.5% Section 301 tariffs on 60 economies both exempt coffee. The remaining cost on non-coffee imports is not disclosed (*estimated* tens of millions per year).
  - The IEEPA refunds are already booked, about ≤$133M one-time in Q3 FY26 (*estimated*).
  - Section 232 tariffs on steel and aluminum (equipment) and furniture remain; they are not sized.
- **Wages:**
  - CA: $16.90 statewide and the $20 fast-food wage, both already absorbed.
  - NY: $17 in NYC and $16 in the rest of the state, CPI-indexed from 2027 under 2023 law.
  - WA: $17.13 statewide and $21.30 in Seattle, both indexed.
  - Starbucks' own Back to Starbucks labor investment already cost about 130 bp of FY25 margin (10-K).
- **Federal law (P.L. 119-21):** SNAP cuts, the no-tax-on-tips deduction (worker-side) and the tax changes. These affect cash taxes more than operating income.

## Biggest uncertainties
1. **The enactment odds on the wage channels are guesses.** The NYC 15% and CA council 40% figures have no market behind them, and they drive about 90% of the expected value. The NYC store count (about 280) and pay level ($22.50) are also *estimated*.
2. **Coffee tariff coverage.** I took the coffee exemptions from secondary legal and trade summaries plus the Federal Register's "for example, coffee" language. Line-level HTS 0901 coverage was not checked. If the exemption were narrowed, the cost could be about $55M/yr, but that is presidential discretion, not an election result.
3. **Starbucks' non-coffee import base and its US hours and wage mix are not disclosed.** The labor formulas use an *estimated* 20 partners per store, 1,144 h/yr each and about $18.5/h.
4. **Union path.** Whether the 2026 bargaining produces a contract without legislation was not found. A voluntary contract would shrink channel 3.
