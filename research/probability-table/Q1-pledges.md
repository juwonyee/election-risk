# Q1: How often do elected executives fulfil campaign pledges?

Research date: 2026-10-24. Scope: US presidents, US governors, comparable executives abroad. Splits asked: (a) unified vs divided government, (b) legislation vs executive action, (c) prominence of the pledge.

Terms used below:
- **"At least partly" (academic):** fully plus partially fulfilled, within the government term (CPPP / Thomson et al. coding). "Partial" means some final action was taken but it fell short of the pledge. A bill that was only introduced, or passed only one chamber, counts as **not** fulfilled.
- **"Kept" (PolitiFact):** "fulfilled, or largely fulfilled", judged on outcomes, not effort. "Compromise" means substantially less than promised but a significant step toward the goal. Broken = not achieved, for any reason, Congress or courts included.
- "Computed" means I worked the number out from counts the source reports. Nothing below is estimated.

## 2. The 3-5 numbers most usable for our table

1. **Base rate, president's party, US, 4-year term: 64% at least partly, 47% fully** (A15; Royed et al. 2019, n=1,078 platform pledges, 1977-2000). The comparative dataset gives **62%** for narrowly defined US pledges (A13). For a "does the policy actually happen" bar, use the **fully** figure. PolitiFact's recent "Kept" rates are much lower (Trump I **24.5%**, Biden **33%**, Obama **48%**: T1, T4, T5) because they track headline promises and grade on outcomes.

2. **Unified vs divided government (legislative pledges):**
   - US academic data: Carter (unified) **65.5%** at least partly / 43.1% fully, vs fully divided terms pooled **63.2%** (computed, A22). That gap is small, and there is only one unified term (n=232).
   - Cross-national: single-party majority **74%** vs single-party minority (US divided coded here) **66%** (A8). After controls the difference is **not significant** (A5: odds ratio 1.46 in the *positive* direction in Model 1, not significant in Model 2). Power-sharing in a coalition matters much more (54%; odds ratio .28-.41).
   - Governor trackers point the other way. Same state, same tracker: Walker (unified) **58% kept** vs Evers (divided) **33% kept** (T7 vs T8).
   - Pick: for a ~2-year legislative-pledge probability, divided government lowers success modestly in the academic data. The tracker evidence suggests a larger penalty for headline pledges.

3. **Executive-only vs legislation: no published fulfilment rate split by this variable was found.** The closest evidence:
   - In Canada, only **22.4%** of pledges (959, 2000-19) generated any bill, so most pledges are fulfilled without legislation (A29).
   - An unreviewed undergraduate thesis reports that 18.75% (Trump) vs 33.33% (Biden) of promises were realised legislatively (A33).
   - PolitiFact's qualitative verdict on Trump I: he did best "in areas that he controlled on his own" (T4).
   - Use a proxy: status-quo pledges, which need no new action, run **82-89%** (A4, A11), against **43%** for change pledges (A11). This bounds the gap between "easy" and "hard" pledges but is not an executive-vs-legislative split.

4. **Prominence (top vs minor pledges):**
   - Weighting UK pledges by how central voters think they are **cuts measured fulfilment by 21 percentage points** (A32).
   - Obama's Top-25 midway through his tenure: 36% kept vs 38% for all pledges (T2 vs T3, same date, n=25).
   - PolitiFact's ~100 headline-promise trackers show 24-33% kept, vs 48% on the 533-item Obameter.
   - A 44-study review finds that salience does **not** increase fulfilment (A34).
   - Pick: prominent pledges are fulfilled **at or below** the overall rate, never above it. Do not apply a prominence bonus.

5. **Timing / horizon:** I found **no** published share fulfilled within the first 2 years. All academic rates are measured over a full term (4 years in the US). The Polimeter states without numbers that pledges drive the first two years and the pace slows in year three (A31 source). Canadian work finds fulfilment is more likely early in a mandate (Pétry & Duval 2018, abstract-level only).

## 3. Caveats

- **Definition gap.** Academic "at least partly fulfilled" counts partial action as success. PolitiFact "Kept" requires the outcome to be mostly achieved. The two scales differ by roughly 20-35 points for the same presidents (e.g. US academic 64% vs PolitiFact 24-48%). Choose one definition and use it everywhere.
- **Sample of pledges.** Academic studies code every testable platform pledge, many of them low-profile, including funding continuations and status-quo pledges. Trackers pick headline promises. Candidate "stated policies" in our use case look more like the tracker sample.
- **Period.** The US academic data ends in 2000. Polarisation and filibuster use have risen since. The authors expect lower fulfilment for Obama II, but that is not measured.
- **The unified-government evidence for the US rests on one term** (Carter). Thomson et al. pool US divided government with parliamentary minority governments, which are stronger (they hold the median legislator and benefit from negative investiture rules).
- **Horizon mismatch.** Studies use the full term (4 years in the US, ~4-5 in parliaments). Our window is ~2 years, so these rates are upper bounds for a 2-year window.
- **Governor trackers** are small (n=27-86), stop at various points (some before the term ended), and reflect each newsroom's selection. T7-T10 are not a controlled comparison.
- **Secondary-only items:** Krukones 75% (A26), Fishel (A25), Pomper & Lederman (A24), Mellon unweighted 69% (A32, from a snippet), Ergul thesis (A33, from a snippet, unreviewed, denominators unclear). Paywalled or bot-blocked sources not read in full: Royed 1996 (BJPS), Royed & Borrelli 1997, Mellon et al. 2023, Guinaudeau & Costa 2018 (RFSP), Bouillaud/Guinaudeau/Persico 2017 (France, pledge media visibility). Their Reagan/Carter-Bush numbers are superseded by A14-A21.
- **Not found:** a US governors academic study with rates (Elling 1979, AJPS, on state platforms: no numbers retrieved), and Brown, DeWine, Christie, Cuomo and Kasich final tracker tallies.
