## 2. Numbers most usable for the probability table

1. **A veto, once cast, sticks about 95% of the time.** The 50-state pooled override rate for 2010-2020 is 5.2% (578/11,173, CSG). The median state is 1.5%, and 22 of 50 states had zero overrides. For presidential vetoes since 1993 the override rate is 9.3% (8/86). For a "candidate promises to block X" row, P(veto holds | veto cast) ≈ 0.95 is a reasonable default. This is a pooled base rate, not conditioned on the opposing party holding a supermajority (see 3).
2. **Thresholds matter, and so does legislative math.** Pooled override rates are 7.2% in simple-majority states (20/276), 8.0% in 3/5 states (106/1,326) and 4.7% in 2/3 states (452/9,571). The jump is driven by veto-proof opposition legislatures. Kentucky 2025 overrode ~all 29 vetoes, Indiana ~60% (2012-22), NC 23/28 in 2017-18 while the GOP held a supermajority. **If the opposing party holds a veto-proof majority, the pooled 5% does not apply.** Use the state's own recent record (often 40-100%).
3. **Executives with a firm, personal veto pledge usually follow through.** Under "President would veto" threats, 70.6% of bills that reached the desk were vetoed (12/17). Most threatened bills (75%, 50/67) never passed both chambers at all. Combined, a firm presidential threat ended with the bill not becoming law (never passed or vetoed) in **62 of 67 = 93%** of cases (computed, 2001-2017). Staff-level threats are far weaker (8.3% veto on enrolled bills).
4. **How often a governor vetoes passed bills varies a lot by state.** Michigan 1953-2022 shows 4.6% of passed bills vetoed with an opposite-party legislature vs 2.3% under a trifecta (about 2x). California under a Dem trifecta runs 10-16%. The national 2025-26 rate is 0.4% of *introduced* bills (MultiState, provisional). A general "share of passed bills vetoed under divided vs unified government, all states" figure was **not found**.
5. **Presidential base rates.** 2,599 vetoes in total, 1,066 of them pocket vetoes. 112 overridden (7.3% of regular vetoes). Recent presidents veto only 10-13 bills per term pair, mostly when Congress is controlled by the other party.

## 3. Caveats

- **Selection effects.** Bills are drafted in anticipation of vetoes, and threats kill bills before passage (D3: 75% never passed). So "share of passed bills vetoed" understates the blocking power of the veto, and the override rate is measured only on bills the governor chose to fight.
- **Pooled numbers are dominated by a few states.** CA (1,695), NY (1,049), FL, MD, and ME account for a large share of the 11,173 vetoes. Many of them are routine or technical, and overrides are never attempted. Excluding CA, NY, MD, FL, and NJ, the pooled override rate is 8.1% (511/6,303, computed).
- **CSG counts mix full and line-item/partial vetoes.** Override rates per *bill* would differ.
- **Thresholds vary in their base** (elected members vs present and voting) and by bill type (e.g., Alaska 3/4 for appropriations, special rules in some states for budget bills). OH and RI at 3/5 come from my classification, consistent with the MultiState count of 7. The constitutional text was not fetched.
- **The divided vs unified evidence is mixed.** Michigan shows ~2x the veto rate under opposite-party control. The MultiState 2025-26 data show trifectas with the highest rates nationally, driven by CA and CO. The denominator for Colorado's 7.98% is unclear, so it shouldn't be used as a share of passed bills.
- **Several primary sources were blocked (403/404)**: NCSL, Book of the States tables, senate.gov, Indiana Capital Chronicle, the MultiState 2026 article. Figures from those are taken from search-result snippets and are marked as such. Klarner & Karch, Herzik & Wiggs, and Watson figures were **not found** in accessible form.
- **Data recency.** The CRS veto-threat data cover only 2001-2017, non-appropriations bills, and the report has small internal count inconsistencies (e.g., Bush pre-veto threats 6 vs 7). NC Cooper totals are mid-2023 snapshots. Presidential totals are as of 2026-01-09.
- **Gubernatorial veto-pledge follow-through: no systematic study found.** Use the presidential CRS numbers (D3/D4) as the closest proxy, and treat that use as an assumption.
