## 2. Numbers most usable for a probability table

1. **An AG enforcement case against a company that gets resolved ends in settlement about 80% of the time (single-state) and 100% of the time (multistate). The enforcer wins about 97% of contested cases.** Only 3.7% of single-state UDAP cases were contested, and defendants won 1 of 32 contested cases (Cox, Widman & Totten 2018; n=671 state + 23 multistate cases, 2014). Once a suit is filed, "some resolution against the company" is close to certain. The real uncertainty is timing and size.
2. **Timing: about 28% of litigated state cases take more than 2 years** (same source). For a 2-year window, the chance that a newly filed AG suit is *resolved* by late 2028 is roughly 70% at most. Multistate cases often settle at or near filing, because the investigation happens before the complaint.
3. **Multistate actions against companies run at about 30-40 a year (about 39/yr under Obama; 644 from 2000 to 2019) and are mostly bipartisan.** Partisan gaps are small in consumer protection (3-11 points) and smallest in health-care fraud. An AG of either party joining a large coalition case is common, so a campaign pledge to "go after" an industry already in a multistate investigation is high-probability. A go-it-alone partisan theory is not.
4. **Governor emergency or public-health EOs survive court challenge about 86% of the time.** In federal court, governments lost 14.2% of challenges (Mok & Posner 2022). Mello et al. found plaintiffs won roughly 112 of more than 1,000 challenges. Courts are not the main threat to a governor's EO. Legislatures are: 8 states enacted curbs within about a year in 2020-21.
5. **Suits against the federal government brought by multistate AG coalitions succeed at least partly 60-80% of the time** (Nolette). After a change of party in the AG's office, the reversals that *are* documented hit federal-policy suits and amicus briefs (Michigan withdrew from 28 in one year) and almost never corporate enforcement. **A systematic drop rate was not found.**

## 3. Caveats

- **Campaign promise to filing: not measured anywhere I could find.** Items 1-3 describe what happens *after* a filing. The step from "promised" to "filed" needs another source, or your own coding (for example, Nolette's searchable lists at https://attorneysgeneral.org/; the statistics pages there are images I could not read).
- Cox et al. covers **one year (2014)** of principle-based UDAP cases only, and includes 5 non-AG state consumer agencies. Many single-state cases target small firms or individuals: 51.2% were against individuals or entities with 6 or fewer employees. For big-company targets, the multistate figures (100% settled) are the more relevant base.
- Nolette & Provost count **settled** multistate cases; their dataset is built from settlements. They do not report how many multistate investigations end with nothing.
- The Good Jobs First figures ($100-106B, 644 cases) are from search summaries; the report page returned 403.
- The COVID EO studies cover **emergency public-health orders in a period of high judicial deference** (Jacobson). They may overstate how long non-emergency business EOs (moratoria, price controls, ESG directives) would survive, especially in state courts. The Wisconsin and Michigan supreme courts both struck down governor COVID orders in 2020. A systematic state-court loss rate was **not found**.
- Mok & Posner's abstract gives no n. The Mello et al. denominator ("more than 1,000") is approximate.
- The Ballotpedia EO counts include routine and ceremonial orders. The share that is business-relevant was **not found** outside health (B2).
- Nolette's 60-80% success rate is spoken (podcast), defined loosely ("some success" at any level), and has no published n.
- The DOJ consent-decree percentages (A14) came from a search snippet. I could not see them in the abstract page.
- The evidence on party turnover is anecdotal (3-4 states). Treat any drop-rate number as an assumption, not a statistic.
