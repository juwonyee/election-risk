## 2. Numbers most usable for the probability table

1. **Citizen initiative pass rate: 42% long-run (1904–2021, n=2,653); 51% in 2010–2019 (n=286).** Legislatively referred measures: **~68% (2024 amendments, n=73)**, roughly 60–75% in long-run state histories. Use about 0.45–0.50 for an initiative and about 0.70 for a legislative referral.
2. **Opposite-party House's partisan agenda bill enacted over a veto-wielding President: close to 0.** All 4 opposition-agenda reconciliation bills sent since 1980 were vetoed. Majority agenda items passed without minority support: **4% (10/254)**. Items that are compromised or co-owned with the President: about 4 of 6 in 2007 (when the opposition held both chambers). Suggested split: pure partisan item **~0.05**; negotiable or bipartisan item **~0.3–0.5**.
3. **Cloture success on legislation: 52% (922 votes, 1991–2022).** For a bill that gets a cloture vote, it became law **45%** of the time (13/29, 117th Congress).
4. **Output under divided government: ~10 important laws per Congress (1991–2024) vs ~13 under unified control.** Mayhew 1947–90: 11.7 vs 12.8. Divided government trims output about 10–20%; it doesn't stop it.
5. **Reconciliation under a House-only flip: 0 enacted since 1987.** A policy that depends on reconciliation is effectively ~0 if the 2026 result is a split Congress.

## 3. Caveats

- **Initiative rates vary a lot by state and year.** Nevada runs the opposite way. The 2016 and 2020 rates were high (66% and 74%). Ballotpedia's three-odd-year figure (41.7% initiatives) doesn't say which years it covers.
- **IRI counts only initiatives.** Popular referendums and legislative referrals are excluded. The IRI table title says 1900, but the data start in 1904.
- **Governor-backed pass rate is not found.** The only evidence is survey experiments on 3 California measures. Don't put a specific number on it.
- **Mayhew's method changed after 1990.** Later Congresses use only Sweep One (journalists' wrap-ups). Mayhew himself notes the wrap-up genre "more or less collapsed" around 2014, which may undercount recent Congresses. His criteria leave out debt-ceiling hikes and routine appropriations, which is why the 2023 FRA is missing. The 2019–20 count is inflated by 5 COVID acts.
- **Binder's series ends in 2012.** I found no published scores for later Congresses.
- **The C3 cloture rate (47%) mixes legislation and nominations.** After November 2013 nomination cloture is majority-vote, so post-2013 totals aren't comparable. Use Pew's legislation-only 52% instead.
- **The cloture rate is not the same as the share of bills killed by the filibuster.** Many bills never get a cloture vote (leaders don't bring up doomed bills), and one bill can have several votes (e.g. 7 for the FAST Act).
- **B8 and B9 rely on party control that I added.** CRS lists the bills and outcomes but doesn't label who controlled each chamber; that comes from standard history.
- **The CRS list includes a 2026 law.** "Secure America Act (P.L. 119-98), 06-10-26" appears in the July 2026 CRS edition. I couldn't verify it against any other source.
- **There is no clean statistic for the share of a House majority's agenda enacted under an opposite-party President.** B12 is case evidence: Six for '06 has n=6, and the rest are illustrative. Curry & Lee's "about half" covers all majorities, not just divided government.
- **Some sources were blocked and not worked around:** senate.gov (403), congress.gov (403), GovTrack (bot challenge), CNN (451), and the full Star Tribune page (429).
