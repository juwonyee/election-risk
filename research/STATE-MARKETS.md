# State-level prediction markets: 2026 midterm catalog

Built 2026-10-15 (Election Day 2026-11-03). Method: rebuilt the Kalshi open-event index (`python scripts/find_markets.py --refresh`, 14,678 open events) and filtered it by state names, office names (governor, attorney general, legislature, commissions, mayors) and policy keywords (ballot/measure/proposition/amendment, minimum wage, property/income/wealth tax, data center, cannabis, sports betting, utilities). Then I pulled 216 candidate events from the Kalshi API (`/events/<ticker>?with_nested_markets=true`) and read each market's `rules_primary`. (The raw pull is kept locally, not in the repo.)

**Counts:** 187 in-scope events across 48 states, plus 5 multi-state events. Thin markets (event volume under 10,000 contracts): 95 of 187. Another 13 state events were reviewed and set aside because they have no clear business channel, and 10 governor markets for 2028 races are listed separately. Federal and macro markets are out of scope. One data-center ratepayer market (`KXRATEPAYERLAW-26SEP`) turned out to resolve on a federal law and was excluded. States with no in-scope market: New Jersey, West Virginia.

**How to read the prices:** each price is the Kalshi midpoint of yes bid and yes ask on 2026-10-15, shown as %. An `*` means the bid/ask spread is wider than 20 cents, so treat that midpoint as unreliable. Volume is the cumulative `volume_fp` summed over the event's markets. Each "Business channel" entry is a research hypothesis. None of it is a finding, and none of it is a view on any party or candidate. Nothing here is investment advice. Polymarket was not used.

**Kalshi labelling quirks:** `GOVPARTYNH-28` and `GOVPARTYKS-27` are the 2026 New Hampshire and Kansas races (named candidates), even though the ticker suffixes say otherwise. Chicago's 2027 mayoral race has two parallel series (`KXMAYORCHI`, `KXCHICAGOMAYOR`). Maryland Question 3 appears twice (`KXMDQUESTION3-26NOV03` and inside the MD state-measure event).
