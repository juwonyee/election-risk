# McDonald's (MCD): 2026 election exposure
Base: FY2025 revenue $26,885M, operating income $12,393M ([10-K, EDGAR](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/63908/000006390826000035/0000063908-26-000035-index.htm)). Concentration facts (all *verified*, FY2025 10-K):
- 45,356 restaurants worldwide, ~95% franchised (95% in the U.S.). U.S.: 13,706 restaurants.
- U.S. segment operating income $5,808M (47% of total). International Operated Markets $6,382M; IDL & Corporate $203M.
- U.S. franchised sales (not McDonald's revenue) $51,946M. U.S. franchised revenues (rent + royalties + fees) $7,371M = **14.2% of franchisee sales**. U.S. franchised margin $6,078M = 11.7% of franchisee sales.
- U.S. company-operated sales $3,115M, margin only $360M (11.6%; down 14% y/y). Company-operated restaurants worldwide: 2,039; U.S. ~685 (*estimated*: 5% of 13,706).
- Franchised margins = ~90% of restaurant-margin dollars. Company-operated payroll & benefits $2,905M on $9,690M company-operated sales = 30% of sales (global; used as U.S. proxy, *estimated*).
- Derived U.S. average unit volume: ($51,946M + $3,115M) / 13,706 = **$4.02M per restaurant**; payroll per restaurant ~ $4.02M x 30% = **$1.2M** (*estimated*).

**How a wage/demand shock reaches McDonald's vs. franchisees.** Franchisees pay the wages at ~95% of restaurants. McDonald's own P&L sees (a) the full labor cost at its ~5% company-operated units, net of menu-price pass-through, and (b) 14.2% of any change in franchisee *sales dollars* (rent and royalty are a percent of sales). Price pass-through raises sales dollars; lost traffic lowers them. California's $20 fast-food wage (a +25% floor increase) raised food-away-from-home prices 3.3-3.6% ([NBER w34990](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34990)) and cut fast-food employment ~2.7% ([Cato summary of Clemens et al.](https://www.cato.org/research-briefs-economic-policy/did-californias-fast-food-minimum-wage-reduce-employment)); Berkeley IRLE found no job loss (contested). Method assumptions used throughout (*estimated*): company-operated price pass-through 60% (so 40% of the labor cost hits margin); franchisee sales change -1% in the downside case (range -1% to +1%, central ~0).

## Bottom line
- In play (expected): **~ -$6M/yr (0.05% of operating income)**; if everything happens: **~ -$72M/yr (0.6%)** (federal $15 and the three state increases overlap; overlap removed).
- Locked in regardless: Brazil 25% Section 301 tariff exempts beef and coffee (~$0 direct); NLRB 2020 franchisor-friendly joint-employer rule reinstated Feb 2026 (not reversible by Congress without the President); no-tax-on-tips/overtime deductions through 2028 (P.L. 119-21; not sized, small); low-income consumer weakness (management: low-income QSR traffic down nearly double digits) is a macro trend, not an election outcome.
- Card fact: **If every wage hike tied to the 2026 elections happened, McDonald's franchisees would pay roughly $0.8B+ more a year in wages, but McDonald's own profit would move by less than 1%.**
- Strongest R-vs-D contrast: Wisconsin governor. Crowley (D, 80.5%) wants $15 now and $20 by 2030; Tiffany (R) opposes any increase. Georgia is similar (Bottoms $15 vs. Jackson none). Both are blunted by Republican legislatures.

### 1. Pennsylvania $15 (Shapiro)
- Election: GOVPARTYPA-26-D 0.98 mid (vol 205,086); R 0.018 (vol 492,426).
- Policy: Shapiro's FY2026-27 budget proposed $15; the D House passed $11/$13/$15 phase-in 104-95 in March 2026; every Senate Republican voted against bringing it up in July 2026 ([Sun-Gazette](https://www.sungazette.com/news/business/2026/03/state-house-approves-boost-in-minimum-wage-to-15/), [Capital-Star](https://penncapital-star.com/videos/the-pennsylvania-capital-stars-week-in-focus-july-3-2026/), [Northeast Times](https://northeasttimes.com/2026/02/06/shapiro-53-billion-budget-min-wage/)).
- Enactment: 15% *estimated*: Republicans hold the Senate and it is unlikely to flip; leaders have signaled a "middle ground" (lower than $15) is possible, so P(some increase) is higher than P($15).
- Size/exposure: PA fast-food mean wage $11.25 (BLS May 2021, [OEWS](https://www.bls.gov/oes/2021/may/oes353023.htm)); assume labor bill +8% at $15 (*estimated*, range 5-12%). PA McDonald's ~482 (xmap.ai 2025, third-party, *estimated*, [xmap](https://www.xmap.ai/blog/how-many-mcdonalds-restaurants-are-in-the-united-states)).
- Dollars: company-op `482 x 5% x $1.2M x 8% x 40% unrecovered = $0.9M`; franchise `482 x 95% x $4.02M x -1% sales x 14.2% = $2.6M`; **if it happens -$3.5M**; expected `-$3.5M x 0.98 x 0.15 = -$0.5M`. Franchisee wage cost (before price increases): `458 x $1.2M x 8% = $44M/yr`.

### 2. Wisconsin $15 -> $20 (Crowley)
- Election: GOVPARTYWI-26-D 0.805 (vol 524,156); R Tiffany 0.205 (vol 825,464).
- Policy: Crowley: $15 immediately, $20 by 2030, CPI indexing, repeal local preemption; Tiffany opposes raising the minimum ([WisPolitics](https://www.wispolitics.com/2026/crowley-campaign-as-david-crowley-fights-for-wisconsin-workers-tom-tiffany-rejects-raising-minimum-wage/), [Fox6](https://www.fox6now.com/news/wisconsin-governor-race-candidates-spar-act-10-wages)).
- Enactment: 10% *estimated*: needs both chambers; Assembly is Republican and the Senate flip is uncertain (no Kalshi market for WI legislature found).
- Exposure: WI restaurant count not found from a reliable source; ~240 by population scaling (5.9M x ~40.6 per million US average), *estimated*. Labor bill +6% at $15, ~+30% at $20 (*estimated*).
- Dollars: `240 x 5% x $1.2M x 6% x 40% = $0.35M` + `240 x 95% x $4.02M x 1% x 14.2% = $1.3M` = **-$1.7M** ($15 step); at $20 with -2% sales: **-$4.3M**. Expected `-$1.7M x 0.805 x 0.10 = -$0.13M`. Franchisee cost $16M/yr ($15), ~$82M/yr ($20).

### 3. Georgia $15 (Bottoms)
- Election: GOVPARTYGA-26-D 0.455 (vol 634,926); R Jackson 0.535 (vol 740,645).
- Policy: Bottoms backs $15 statewide; Jackson emphasizes workforce training, no wage increase ([Atlanta News First transcript](https://www.atlantanewsfirst.com/2026/10/10/read-full-transcript-town-hall-debate-between-keisha-lance-bottoms-rick-jackson/), [Fox5](https://www.fox5atlanta.com/news/keisha-lance-bottoms-georgia-governor-candidate-profile)).
- Enactment: 3% *estimated*: both chambers Republican with wide margins.
- Exposure: GA fast-food mean wage $12.47 (BLS May 2023, [OEWS](https://www.bls.gov/oes/2023/may/oes353023.htm)); labor +10% *estimated*; ~447 restaurants (xmap.ai, *estimated*).
- Dollars: `447 x 5% x $1.2M x 10% x 40% = $1.1M` + `447 x 95% x $4.02M x 1% x 14.2% = $2.4M` = **-$3.5M**; expected `x 0.455 x 0.03 = -$0.05M`. Franchisee cost $51M/yr.

### 4. Federal minimum wage
- Election: House D ~90%, Senate D ~62% (brief). Any federal law needs the President's signature (term to Jan 2029).
- Enactment: 3% *estimated* (no market; President has not endorsed; veto override not feasible).
- Exposure: share of U.S. McDonald's in states with floors below $15 ~50% (*estimated*; ~20 states remain at $7.25). Labor +8% avg.
- Dollars: `6,853 x 5% x $1.2M x 8% x 40% = $13M` + `6,853 x 95% x $4.02M x 1% x 14.2% = $37M` = **-$50M**; expected -$1.5M. Franchisee cost ~$630M/yr. Overlaps channels 1-3 (removed from the total).

### 5. California Fast Food Council
- Election: GOVPARTYCA-26-D 0.963 (vol 487,068); R 0.038 (vol 1,402,961). The governor appoints most council members.
- Policy: AB 1228 lets the council raise the $20 wage annually by the lesser of 3.5% or CPI-W, through 2029; a 3.5% COLA ($20.70) was floated in 2025 but the council went dormant ([Restaurant Business](https://restaurantbusinessonline.com/workforce/californias-fast-food-council-has-fallen-dormant), [CalMatters](https://calmatters.org/california-divide/2025/03/california-fast-food-council-one-year/)).
- Enactment: 35% under D, 10% under R by end-2027 (*estimated*; the council has not acted in 18+ months).
- Exposure: CA restaurants ~1,500 (population-scaled, *estimated*); labor +3.5%; franchise sales -0.3% (*estimated*, scaled from AB 1228 evidence).
- Dollars: `75 x $1.2M x 3.5% x 40% = $1.3M` + `1,425 x $4.02M x 0.3% x 14.2% = $2.4M` = **-$3.7M**; expected `-$3.7M x (0.963x0.35 + 0.038x0.10) = -$1.3M`. D-vs-R difference ~$0.9M. Franchisee cost ~$60M/yr.

### 6. NYC $30

Current NYC wage $17.
- Exposure: NYC McDonald's ~200 (*estimated*, not found from a primary source); labor +70% at $30; franchise sales -6% (*estimated*).
- Dollars: `10 x $1.2M x 70% x 40% = $3.4M` + `190 x $4.02M x 6% x 14.2% = $6.5M` = **-$9.9M**; expected -$0.7M. Franchisee cost ~$160M/yr at $30.

### 7. SNAP lapse / shutdown
- Market: KXGOVTSHUTDOWN-26DEC12 0.33 (vol 15,946). SNAP is funded through 2026-12-11 only (brief). The election shapes lame-duck bargaining only indirectly.
- Policy/size: SNAP costs ~$8.2B/month in benefits; in Nov 2025 courts forced partial payment (50-65%) from contingency funds ([DTN](https://www.dtnpf.com/agriculture/web/ag/news/article/2025/10/30/federal-judge-weighing-fate-november), [San Angelo Live](https://sanangelolive.com/node/104072)).
- Exposure: McDonald's said low-income QSR traffic fell nearly double digits and cited SNAP worries during the 2025 shutdown ([Las Vegas Sun/WaPo](https://lasvegassun.com/news/2025/nov/19/mcdonalds-is-losing-its-low-income-customers-econo/)); low-income guests "still declining" in Q1 2026 ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/earnings/2026/mcdonalds-ceo-consumer-caution-drives-demand-value/)).
- Dollars (*estimated*): `$8.2B x 50% unpaid x 15% shifted out of eating out x 60% limited-service x 14% MCD share = $52M systemwide sales`; `$49M x 14.2% + $2.6M x 50% = $8.3M` per month of lapse. Expected `-$8.3M x 0.33 x 0.8 = -$2.2M`.

### 8. Beef/coffee tariffs
- Brazil 25% Section 301 (from 2026-07-22) keeps beef and coffee exempt ([Pakistan Today/Reuters](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/2026/07/17/us-imposes-25percent-tariffs-on-brazilian-goods-keeps-exemptions-for-beef-coffee-and-aircraft), [WLJ](https://www.wlj.net/trump-imposes-tariffs-on-brazil-exempts-beef/)). Whether the separate forced-labor 301 tariffs cover Brazilian or other beef: not found.
- Congress cannot undo 301 tariffs without a veto-proof law (brief), so the election does not move this. KXTARIFFRATEBR-27JAN01 is **thin** (vol 0-66). In play: ~$0.

### 9. Joint employer
- NLRB withdrew the vacated 2023 rule and reinstated the 2020 "substantial direct and immediate control" standard on 2026-02-27 ([Sullivan & Cromwell](https://www.sullcrom.com/insights/blogs/2026/March/NLRB-Reinstates-Narrower-Joint-Employer-Standard-First-Implemented-During-President-Trump-First-Term)). DOL proposed an FLSA joint-employer rule in April 2026. Board composition follows the President, not the midterms. Not in play before 2029.

## Locked in regardless
- **Joint employer:** 2020 NLRB standard in force (favorable to franchisors); not sized.
- **Tariffs:** Brazil 25% 301 with beef/coffee exempt; Section 232 steel/aluminum (equipment, packaging) in force. Direct food-cost effect ~$0 (not sized for packaging).
- **No tax on tips/overtime (P.L. 119-21, 2025-2028):** small worker-income boost; most QSR crew are not tipped. Not sized (JCT figure not pulled).
- **SNAP cuts in P.L. 119-21** and continuing low-income traffic weakness: macro headwind to franchisee sales; not attributable to the election.
- **Already-scheduled state increases** (CA $20 fast-food, NY $17/$16 indexed, MI/AZ indexed) continue whoever wins.

## Biggest uncertainties
1. **Franchisee sales response to wage hikes** (-1% to +1%). This one assumption drives most of each state figure. Price pass-through can raise rent/royalty dollars even while franchisee margins fall.
2. **State restaurant counts** (third-party aggregators disagree by 20-50%) and wage-bill increases (BLS state means are 2021/2023 vintage).
3. **Legislative enactment odds** (PA Senate, WI Assembly, GA): no prediction markets; all *estimated*.
4. **SNAP pass-through** to restaurant spending is a rough assumption; SNAP cannot be spent at restaurants directly.
5. Second-order risk not sized: franchisee financial stress leading to slower remodels or new-unit development (McDonald's funds much of this).
