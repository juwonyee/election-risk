# Lennar (LEN): 2026 election exposure
Base: FY2025 (ended 2025-11-30) revenue **$34.19B**, earnings before income taxes **$2.81B** (EDGAR `IncomeLossFromContinuingOperationsBeforeIncomeTaxes...`, 10-K accession 0001628280-26-003870, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/920760/000162828026003870/0001628280-26-003870-index.htm). Lennar has not tagged `OperatingIncomeLoss` since FY2020, so **pre-tax earnings of $2.81B is used as the operating-income denominator** (homebuilding operating earnings $3.015B + Financial Services $0.61B - Multifamily $0.075B - corporate G&A $0.64B ≈ $2.9B; 10-K segment table). *Verified.*
- 82,583 homes delivered in FY2025, average price $391,000. Segments (10-K/8-K, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/920760/000162828025057408/ex991-20251130x8kq4.htm):
  - **East (Florida, NJ, PA):** 18,938 deliveries, $6.90B home revenue, gross margin 19.8%, **operating earnings $704M** (23% of homebuilding).
  - **South Central (Texas, AR, KS, MO, OK):** 23,416 deliveries, average price $238K, **operating earnings $493M** (16%).
  - Central (GA, NC, SC, TN, VA/MD and others): $668M. West (AZ, CA, CO, NV and others): $1,116M.
- FY2026 is running much lower: 9-month homebuilding operating earnings were $1.36B, with East at $479M (35%) (10-Q for 2026-08-31, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/920760/000162828026064557/len-20260831.htm). FY2026 delivery guidance has been cut to 80,000-81,000. On percentages: dollar impacts below are shown as a share of FY2025 pre-tax income, so they would be a larger share of FY2026 income.
- The 10-Q calls Texas and Florida "its two largest states" (resale competition is rising in both) and says labor is "more constrained ... reflecting immigration enforcement activity". Florida's share of East deliveries is not disclosed. *Estimated* at ~85% (≈16,100 homes), because NJ and PA are small divisions. Texas's share of South Central is *estimated* at ~75% (≈17,600 homes).

## Bottom line
- **In play (expected): ~$36M/yr (≈1.3% of FY2025 pre-tax income).** If everything happens (central cases): ~$365M/yr (≈13%), with a wide range.
- **Locked in regardless:** Section 232 wood tariffs (est. ~$78M/yr now, ~$108M/yr after cabinets/vanities step from 25% to 50% on 2027-01-01). OBBBA multi-year immigration-enforcement funding, which is already pushing up labor costs (not separately quantified by Lennar). ROAD to Housing Act institutional-investor ban, with build-to-rent exempt. Texas 2025 homestead exemption raised to $140K.
- **Card fact:** Lennar's biggest cost pressures (wood tariffs and an immigration crackdown on construction labor) are already locked in whoever wins; the ballot items that most affect it, Florida's property-tax cut and Texas property-tax plans, are worth only tens of millions.
- **Strongest R-vs-D contrast:** The two sides move different channels. A Republican result in Florida/Texas favors homestead property-tax cuts, a small demand tailwind (≈+$20-75M if enacted). A Democratic House could trim ICE appropriations, giving labor-cost relief (≈+$180M if enacted), but that has a low chance of enactment because OBBBA already funded enforcement and the President would veto.

### 1. Florida Amendment 3
- **Odds:** Kalshi KXFLPROPERTYTAX-26NOV03, midpoint 0.41, volume 41,725, as of 2026-10-14. The Republican Party of Florida, the governor and Florida Realtors support it. The Florida Democratic Party, cities and counties oppose it (https://floridaphoenix.com/2026/06/25/campaign-launches-to-oppose-property-tax-cut-constitutional-amendment/).
- **Policy:**
  - The non-school homestead exemption rises to $150K in 2027 and $250K in 2028, then is indexed.
  - The $25K school exemption stays.
  - People not permanently resident by 2026-12-31 get only a $50K non-school exemption until their fifth year.
  - The non-homestead assessment cap falls from 10% to 5%.
  - Local property-tax uses are limited to 7 categories.
  - Sources: https://www.floridataxwatch.org/research/florida-property-tax-resource-center/, https://www.bilzin.com/insights/publications/2026/09/floridas-amendment-3-residency-deadline
- **Size (EDR, state economists):** local revenue loss of ≈$5B in FY2027-28, $8.8B in year 2, $9.7B in year 3, $10.75B in year 4. Up to ~$12B recurring at full implementation (https://capitalanalyticsassociates.com/florida-property-tax-fight-tests-homeowner-relief-limits/). House analysts put first-year non-school losses at $4.6B. Cities and counties may raise fees and assessments to offset, but no estimates exist (https://www.wlrn.org/light/government-politics/2026-07-31/floridas-proposed-property-tax-amendment-will-require-trade-offs-some-may-end-up-with-higher-costs, https://www.wdbo.com/news/local/could-florida-homeowners-property-tax-savings-be-offset-by-new-fees-heres-what-officials-say/3PWMNGJM35DN3LZWA6TC34DZCU/).
- **Mechanism for Lennar:**
  - Buyers who already live in Florida get roughly $200K more non-school exemption.
  - In-migrants (a large part of Florida new-home demand) get no change from today's ~$50K for 4 years. Relative to the status quo they are neutral, not worse off.
  - Resale and new homes are treated the same, so this is a broad demand effect, not a shift between new and existing homes.
  - Offsets: new local fees, special assessments or impact fees on new development. Florida's 2021 HB 337 caps impact-fee increases, which limits how fast fees can rise.
  - Possible one-time pull-forward of in-migrant purchases before 2026-12-31 (not quantified).
- **Formula (*estimated*):**
  - Demand side: `16,100 FL deliveries × 50% existing-resident buyers × ($200K extra exemption × 10 non-school mills = $2,000/yr ÷ 0.08 mortgage constant = $25K purchasing power) × 25% captured via lower incentives = 8,050 × $6,250 = +$50M/yr` (at full 2028 phase-in).
  - Offset: `16,100 × ~$2,000 new fees/assessments absorbed = -$32M` (range $0 to -$80M).
  - **Net +$18M** (range -$80M to +$100M).
  - Expected: `$18M × 0.41 × 0.95 = $7M`.
  - The 50% resident share, 10 mills and 25% capture are assumptions. Lennar does not disclose buyer origin.

### 2. Texas governor and property tax
- **Odds:** GOVPARTYTX-26, Abbott 0.805 midpoint (vol 706,973), Hinojosa 0.195 (vol 1,499,031).
- **Abbott plan:** appraisal cap cut from 10% to 3%, extended to all property; a path to eliminate homestead school M&O taxes through a constitutional amendment; possibly a higher sales tax. Nothing substantive can pass before the 2027 session. A full school-tax replacement would have cost $39.5B in tax year 2023 (Legislative Budget Board) (https://www.hppr.org/hppr-news/2025-12-10/texas-gov-greg-abbott-has-a-sweeping-plan-to-abolish-school-property-taxes-would-it-work, https://www.fox26houston.com/news/gov-abbott-targets-out-of-control-property-taxes-new-five-point-tax-relief-plan).
- **Hinojosa plan:** a one-time $1,500-per-household rebate costing $17B (https://www.texastribune.org/2026/07/07/gina-hinojosa-proposes-sending-every-texas-household-1500-in-her-bid-to-oust-greg-abbott/).
- **Enactment (*estimated*):** 25% under Abbott and 15% under Hinojosa. A joint resolution needs two-thirds of the Legislature plus voter approval in Nov 2027 but no governor signature, so the governor matters less than usual. Cost and sales-tax trade-offs make full elimination unlikely in one session.
- **Formula (*estimated*):** `17,600 TX deliveries × ($238K price - $140K exemption = $98K × ~1.0% school rate ≈ $1,000/yr ÷ 0.08 = $12.5K purchasing power × 25% capture = $3,125) = +$55M/yr`.
  - Expected: `$55M × 0.805 × 0.25 = $11M` (Abbott path) and `$55M × 0.195 × 0.15 = $1.6M` (Hinojosa path).

### 3. Immigration enforcement and labor
- **Odds:** House D ~90% (brief). Kalshi KXDEPORTATIONS-27JAN01 prices 2026 removals at 400K-450K: >400K at 0.97 (vol 26,751); >450K at 0.37 (vol 28,110); >500K at 0.065 (vol 57,814). The 2026 total is essentially set before the new Congress sits.
- **Policy:** OBBBA multi-year enforcement funding is law. A Democratic House could limit FY2027+ ICE/worksite enforcement through appropriations, but would face a veto and shutdown leverage.
- **Exposure:**
  - Lennar subcontracts "virtually all" construction (10-K).
  - The Q3 FY2026 10-Q cites labor constraints from immigration enforcement adding cost pressure.
  - Immigrant share of construction workers: Florida 40.6%, Texas 39%, US 26.3% (NAHB, https://eyeonhousing.org/2026/04/which-states-and-construction-trades-depend-the-most-on-immigrant-workers).
  - Construction cost is ~$80/sq ft (10-Q).
- **Formula (*estimated*):** `80,500 homes × (2,100 sq ft × $80 = $168K direct cost × 45% labor = $75.6K) × 3% enforcement-driven wage premium avoided = $2,270/home = $183M` (range 1%-5%: $61M-$304M).
  - Expected: `$183M × 0.90 × 0.10 = $16M`.

### 4. Section 232 wood tariffs
- **In force:**
  - Softwood lumber: 10%.
  - Kitchen cabinets/vanities: 25%, scheduled to rise to 50% on 2027-01-01.
  - Upholstered furniture: 25%, scheduled to rise to 30%.
  - The increase was delayed by the 2025-12-31 proclamation (https://www.thompsonhinesmartrade.com/2026/01/president-trump-delays-section-232-tariff-increase-on-wood-furniture-cabinets-and-vanities/, https://mohawkglobal.com/word-from-the-white-house/furniture-and-cabinet-imports-section-232-tariff-increase-delayed-to-2027/).
- **Context:** NAHB's April 2025 builder survey put the typical tariff cost at $10,900/home. That figure included the IEEPA tariffs, which were later struck down (https://www.nahb.org/advocacy/top-priorities/material-costs/how-tariffs-impact-home-building). Lennar does not quantify its tariff cost.
- **Formula (*estimated*):**
  - Lumber: `80,500 × $20K softwood/home × 30% imported × 10% = $48M`.
  - Cabinets/vanities now: `80,500 × $5K × 30% imported × 25% = $30M`.
  - 2027 step-up: `+$30M`.
  - Total **$108M**. Expected: `× 0.61 D sweep × 0.03 veto-proof = $2M`.

## Locked in regardless
- **Section 232 wood products:** ~$78M/yr now, ~$108M/yr from 2027 (*estimated*, see channel 4). Steel, aluminum and copper Section 232 rates (10-50%) and the Section 301 tariffs on 60 economies also apply to appliances, fixtures and HVAC (not quantified).
- **OBBBA (P.L. 119-21, https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/1):** multi-year immigration-enforcement funding, which feeds the labor-cost pressure Lennar cites. Its homeowner tax provisions (mortgage interest deduction and SALT changes) are also locked.
- **21st Century ROAD to Housing Act (law 2026-07-11):**
  - Bans for-profit owners of 350 or more single-family homes from new purchases, starting about 180 days after enactment (~Jan 2027).
  - Build-to-rent programs are exempt, and the seven-year sell-off rule was dropped.
  - This limits bulk sales of completed spec homes to large investors but keeps Lennar's build-to-rent channel open.
  - Sources: https://www.mayerbrown.com/de/insights/publications/2026/07/housing-legislation-banning-large-institutional-investors-from-purchasing-single-family-homes-becomes-law, https://www.housingwire.com/articles/house-exempts-btr-road-to-housing/
- **Texas Proposition 13 (Nov 2025):** school homestead exemption raised from $100K to $140K, with a $2.7B state backfill in 2026-27 (https://www.fox26houston.com/election/texas-proposition-13-election-results-2025, https://www.ownwell.com/blog/texas-proposition-13-homestead-exemption).

## Biggest uncertainties
1. **Florida Amendment 3 sign and size:** Lennar does not disclose the share of buyers who already live in Florida. The 25% capture and the local-fee offset are assumptions. The net effect could plausibly be negative if counties lean on new-development fees and assessments.
2. **Labor-cost elasticity to enforcement:** no public estimate links enforcement levels to residential subcontractor wages. Lennar reports rising labor costs but no figure. The 2,100 sq ft home size and 45% labor share are *estimated*.
3. **Florida/Texas delivery shares:** these are estimated from segment totals (85% and 75%). State-level deliveries are not disclosed.
4. **FY2026 earnings are falling** (9-month homebuilding operating earnings of $1.36B against $3.0B for all of FY2025). Percentages of FY2025 income understate the relative weight.
5. **Not quantified:** a federal shutdown on Dec 12 (~33%) could delay FHA/USDA mortgage processing for Lennar buyers; mortgage-rate effects of election outcomes are excluded.
