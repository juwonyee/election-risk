# Uber Technologies (UBER): 2026 election exposure

Base: FY2025 revenue **$52,017M**, operating income **$5,565M** (10-K, EDGAR accession 0001543151-26-000015, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1543151/000154315126000015/0001543151-26-000015-index.htm), *verified*.
Concentration (*verified*, 10-K, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1543151/000154315126000015/uber-20251231.htm):
- United States revenue $23,771M (45.7%). US&Canada $26,469M. UK $10,609M.
- Segments: Mobility revenue $29,670M, segment Adj. EBITDA $7,899M. Delivery $17,248M / $3,572M. Freight $5,099M / -$33M.
- Mobility Gross Bookings 2025: $21,182M + $23,762M + $25,111M + $27,442M = **$97,497M**. Mobility take rate = 29,670 / 97,497 = 30.4%.
- 15% of Mobility Gross Bookings start or end at an airport. Insurance expense rose $851M in 2025. The 10-K says insurance costs are higher in the US and Canada than elsewhere.
- The 10-K does not split Mobility by state. State shares below are *estimated*.

Shared assumptions (*estimated*, used in every channel):
- US Mobility GB ≈ $97,497M x 45.7% (US share of total revenue, used as a proxy) = **$44.6B**.
- Driver earnings ≈ 70% of GB (1 - 30.4% take rate).
- A collective-bargaining or pay mandate adds **+5%** to driver pay.
- Uber absorbs **50%** of the added cost and passes the rest through to fares.

## Bottom line
- **In play (expected): ~$10M/yr (≈0.2% of operating income). If everything happens: ~$810M/yr (≈15%).** About 96% of the if-everything figure is a federal gig-labor law, which the President would veto.
- **Locked in regardless:**
  - California: AB 1340 lets drivers unionize; SB 371 cuts uninsured/underinsured motorist (UM/UIM) coverage from $1M to $60k/$300k; SB 623 benchmarks crash medical damages from 1/1/2027. Together they ended the Nov 2026 ballot fight.
  - Massachusetts: 2024 Question 3 union (certified May 2026) plus a $32.50/hr engaged-time pay floor.
  - Illinois: HB 5090 union law, signed Aug 2026, with a $0.04/ride fee.
  - Minnesota: 2024 pay floor of $1.28/mile + $0.31/min.
  - NYC: TLC minimum pay.
  - Federal: DOL independent-contractor rulemaking is executive action and runs to 2029 whatever the result.
  - None of these change with the Nov 3 result.
- **Card fact:** Uber's big California ballot fight over crash-lawsuit fees was settled by a law in June. Nothing on the Nov 3 ballots directly targets Uber.
- **Strongest R-vs-D contrast:** The Nevada governor race (Ford-D 51.5% vs. Lombardo-R 48.5%). A Democratic win gives a D trifecta with a legislature that is ~94% D. That could pass driver-labor or rideshare-liability bills that Lombardo would likely veto. Federally, a D Congress could pass a PRO-Act-style ABC test, but it hits a presidential veto through Jan 2029.

Checked and found **not in play** (no Uber-relevant measure, or the outcome is near-certain and the policy is already law):
- California Nov 2026 propositions: the Uber fee-cap and trial-lawyer rideshare-liability measures were replaced by SB 623. Kalshi KXCABALLOTMEASURES-26NOV03 lists no rideshare prop.

- Nevada: the Uber 20% fee-cap initiative was struck by the NV Supreme Court in Jan 2025, then followed by a 2025 compromise law.
- Colorado, Washington and Arizona ballot lists: no gig measure found.
- Governors of CA (Becerra 0.963), NY (Hochul 0.940), IL (Pritzker 0.967) and MA (Healey 0.948): the outcomes are near-certain and the gig policies are already enacted.

### 1. Federal gig-labor law
- **Election:** House D ~90%, Senate D ~62%, D sweep ~61% (Kalshi, per BRIEF context).
- **Policy:** House/Senate Democrats' PRO Act would apply an ABC test under the NLRA. Separately, DOL proposed in Feb 2026 to restore the 2021 independent-contractor test, which favors contractor status (https://www.gibsondunn.com/us-department-of-labor-initiates-rulemaking-to-readopt-its-2021-interpretation-of-who-qualifies-as-independent-contractor-under-flsa/). The DOL rule is executive and does not depend on the midterms.
- **Enactment:** 0.01 *estimated*. The President serves to Jan 2029, and peak GOP crossover so far (5 senators / 6 House members) is far short of 2/3.
- **Size:** Modeled as a national bargaining outcome, not full FLSA employee reclassification. The upper bound for reclassification, from Barclays (2019), is ~$3,625/driver/yr in California alone, or ~$725M/yr for Uber CA (https://qz.com/1643263/the-cost-to-uber-and-lyft-if-drivers-were-employees ; https://www.verdict.co.uk/california-driver-ruling-uber-lyft/).
- **Formula:** US Mobility GB $44.6B x 70% driver share x 5% pay uplift x 50% absorbed = **$781M/yr**.
- **Expected:** $781M x 0.61 x 0.01 = **$4.8M**.

### 2. Nevada governor (with a near-certain D legislature)
- **Election:** GOVPARTYNV-26, Ford (D) mid 0.515 (bid 0.51/ask 0.52, vol 961,505, 2026-10-14). Cook rates it a toss-up (https://www.cookpolitical.com/governor/race/479546). NV Senate D 0.94 and Assembly D 0.945 (thin), so P(D trifecta) ≈ 0.515 x 0.94 = 0.48.
- **Policy:**
  - Uber spent heavily in Nevada on tort/fee caps. Its 20% fee-cap initiative was struck by the NV Supreme Court on 2025-01-27 (https://thenevadaindependent.com/article/uber-backed-ballot-petition-capping-attorney-fees-blocked-by-nevada-supreme-court).
  - The 2025 Legislature then passed a compromise bill, which Lombardo signed.
  - An Uber-affiliated PAC (Nevadans for Fair Recovery) gave $250,000 to Lombardo-allied committees (https://www.newsfromthestates.com/article/one-safe-bet-race-governor-lombardo-wont-be-outspent).
  - Under a D trifecta, *estimated* risk: driver bargaining (the MA, CA and IL template) or expanded TNC liability. Under R, the status quo holds.
- **Enactment:** 0.30 *estimated*. The legislature meets only in odd years (2027 session), and trial-lawyer and labor priorities compete with the 2025 compromise.
- **Exposure:** NV share of US Mobility GB of 2.5% is *estimated*. Las Vegas is airport- and Strip-heavy, and 15% of global Mobility GB is airport trips (10-K).
- **Formula:** $44.6B x 2.5% x 70% x 5% x 50% = **$19.5M/yr**.
- **Expected:** $19.5M x 0.48 x 0.30 = **$2.8M**.

### 3. Minnesota trifecta
- **Election:**
  - GOVPARTYMN-26 Klobuchar (D) 0.9505 (vol 208,219).
  - KXSTATELEG-MNHOUSE26 D 0.885 (vol 10,693). The chamber is currently tied 67-67 (https://mprnews.org/story/2026/09/29/minnesota-legislative-races-to-watch-in-the-state-house-senate).
  - KXSTATELEG-MNSEN26 D 0.835 (vol 3,427, thin).
  - P(D trifecta): 0.70 if the races were independent, ≤0.835 if they are fully correlated. Using **0.75** *estimated*.
- **Policy:** The 2024 law set a $1.28/mile + $0.31/min floor (https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/minnesota-governor-signs-rideshare-bill-uber-lyft/). That floor is locked in. A trifecta could add a bargaining law like Illinois HB 5090 (https://www.nprillinois.org/illinois/2026-08-10/rideshare-unionization-drug-pricing-oversight-plastic-pellet-and-light-pollution-bills-become-law). No 2026 MN bill text was found.
- **Enactment:** 0.35 *estimated*. Bargaining could also yield further pay gains on top of the 2024 floor.
- **Exposure:** MN share of US Mobility GB of 1.2% is *estimated* (1.7% of US population, adjusted down for lower rideshare density).
- **Formula:** $44.6B x 1.2% x 70% x 5% x 50% = **$9.4M/yr**.
- **Expected:** $9.4M x 0.75 x 0.35 = **$2.5M**.

## Locked in regardless
- **California package (2025-26):**
  - AB 1340 lets drivers unionize.
  - SB 371 cuts the TNC UM/UIM requirement from $1M to $60k/$300k from 2026-01-01 (https://smallbiztrends.com/california-passes-sb-371-to-cut-rideshare-insurance-costs-benefit-drivers/). Sponsors say insurance is about one-third of each CA fare. Uber's dollar savings were not found.
  - SB 623, chaptered 2026-06-25, sets a 70th-percentile billed-charge benchmark for lien-based medical damages in rideshare crash claims from 2027-01-01. It ended the dueling Nov 2026 initiatives (https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/07/california-enacts-sb-623).
  - Size: favorable to insurance costs, but no public estimate was found.
- **Massachusetts:**
  - The 2024 Question 3 union, App Drivers Union, was certified May 2026 for ~70,000 drivers. A contract is pending.
  - The 2024 AG settlement sets a $32.50/hr engaged-time floor (https://allwork.space/2026/05/uber-and-lyft-drivers-unionize-in-massachusetts-in-first-for-u-s-ride-share-gig-workers/).
  - Gov. Healey is 0.948 to win, and the legislature is D.
- **Illinois:** HB 5090 (signed Aug 2026) allows union certification at 30% of drivers and imposes a $0.04/ride fee that Uber cannot pass through. Cost = 4¢ x IL Uber trips (not found). At an *estimated* 100M trips that is ~$4M/yr.
- **Minnesota:** 2024 pay floor of $1.28/mi + $0.31/min (above). **NYC:** TLC minimum per-mile/per-minute pay since 2019, which the 10-K cites as an adverse impact.
- **Federal:** The DOL independent-contractor rule is executive action. The 2024 rule is not enforced (May 2025) and a 2021-style rule was proposed Feb 2026. It stays under the current administration through Jan 2029, whatever the midterm result.
- **Autonomous vehicles:**
  - NY: Hochul withdrew her upstate robotaxi pilot in Feb 2026 (https://www.planetizen.com/news/2026/02/136999-hochul-reverses-course-robotaxis).
  - MA: a human-safety-operator bill (S.2393/H.3669) is pending, and no AV ballot question was certified (https://malegislature.gov/Bills/194/S2393.Html).
  - AV rules are set by legislatures whose control is near-certain (NY, MA), or by states that are AV-permissive under both parties (AZ, TX). The near-term P&L effect on Uber is small and not quantified.
- **Insurance cost trend:** +$851M insurance expense in 2025 (10-K). This is driven by the legal environment and is not tied to a Nov 3 race.

## Biggest uncertainties
1. **State Mobility mix is not disclosed.** The NV 2.5% and MN 1.2% shares and the 45.7% US share of Mobility GB are proxies (*estimated*). Error could be ±50%.
2. **Policy content after a trifecta is speculative.** No specific NV or MN bill was found. The 5% pay uplift and 50% absorption are *estimated*. Actual bargaining outcomes (MA, CA and IL contracts are not yet signed) will set the benchmark.
3. **Thin markets.** MN Senate (3,427) and NV Assembly (2,402) volumes are below 10,000.
4. **Federal channel.** The ~$781M is driven entirely by a veto-blocked scenario. Full FLSA reclassification would be larger: Barclays put California alone at ~$725M in 2019.
