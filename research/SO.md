# Southern Company (SO): 2026 election exposure
Base: FY2025 revenue $29,553M, operating income $7,285M, net income $4,341M ([10-K FY2025, EDGAR](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/92122/000009212226000006/0000092122-26-000006-index.htm), *verified*). Segment net income 2025: Georgia Power $2,851M, Alabama Power $1,516M, Mississippi Power $215M, Southern Power $125M, Southern Company Gas $732M, parent/other -$1,098M ([Q4 2025 release](https://southerncompany.mediaroom.com/2026-02-19-Southern-Company-reports-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2025-earnings), *verified*). Georgia Power alone: operating revenue $12,631M, operating expenses $8,792M, so operating income about $3,839M (more than half of SO's total). Common equity was $27,034M at 12/31/2025. 2026 capex is $10.1B of the system's $15.9B ([10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/92122/000009212226000006/so-20251231.htm), *verified*). Georgia Power's base rates are frozen through 2028: ROE set point 10.50%, range 9.50-11.90%, equity ratio 56%. A full rate case may be filed by July 2028 (10-K). Alabama base rates are frozen by state law until 2029-01-01 ([10-Q Q2 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/92122/000009212226000054/so-20260630.htm)). About 16 GW of large-load (mostly data-center) contracts are signed or agreed system-wide (10-Q Q2 2026).

## Bottom line
- **In play (expected): about $135M/yr (about 1.9% of operating income). If everything happens: about $1.0B/yr (about 14%).** Most of it shows up in 2029 or later, because Georgia and Alabama base rates are frozen through 2028.
- **Locked in regardless:** Georgia Power's base-rate freeze through 2028 (ROE 10.5%). Alabama's statutory freeze until 2029. The 9,885 MW Georgia certification (Dec 2025, about $16B). Storm recovery of $156M/yr plus $31M/yr through 2031. OBBB repeal of wind/solar credits for late projects while 45U/45J nuclear PTCs remain. Section 232 steel/aluminum/copper tariffs (10-50%), which are mostly passed through into rate base. EPA's GHG-rule repeal path.
- **Card fact:** If Democrats win both Georgia Public Service Commission seats on Nov 3, they take a 3-2 majority on the board that will set Georgia Power's profit rate in its 2028 rate case. Each half-point cut is worth about $180M a year.
- **Strongest R-vs-D contrast:** Control of the Georgia PSC. A Republican hold keeps the board that approved the 10.5% ROE freeze and the 10 GW data-center build 5-0. A Democratic 3-2 board would be led by commissioners who say Georgia Power's profit margin is "above national averages" and that they are "skeptical" of the data-center build.

### Channel 1: Georgia PSC control and the 2028 rate case (main channel)
- **Election.** Two of five seats are on the statewide ballot on 2026-11-03. District 3 is Peter Hubbard (D, incumbent; elected 2025 for a one-year term) against Fitz Johnson (R, the incumbent Hubbard ousted). District 5 is open: Shelia Edwards (D), Josh Tolbert (R) and Thomas Blooming (L). Pridemore (R) resigned; Gov. Kemp appointed Stan Wise as a placeholder through 12/31 and Wise is not on the ballot. Republicans hold 3-2 today. **If Democrats win both seats they hold 3-2** ([Ballotpedia](https://news.ballotpedia.org/2026/08/10/partisan-control-of-georgia-public-service-commission-at-stake-in-november-election/), [AJC on the Wise appointment](https://www.ajc.com/politics/2026/08/kemp-picks-stan-wise-to-replace-georgia-pscs-tricia-pridemore-who-stepped-down-early/)). Alicia Johnson (D) holds District 2 until 2030 ([Wikipedia 2025 special](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Georgia_Public_Service_Commission_special_election)). A 2026 Democratic sweep would therefore control the board that decides the 2028 rate case. If no D5 candidate reaches 50%, there is a December 1 runoff.
- **Odds.** There is no Kalshi or Polymarket market on the PSC races (Polymarket search found none; no PSC poll found). Proxies:
  - In Nov 2025, Hubbard won 979,335 to 576,941 (62.9%), and Alicia Johnson also won by a landslide ([Cobb Courier](https://cobbcountycourier.com/2025/11/democrats-flip-two-seats-on-the-georgia-public-service-commission/)). That was an off-year election.
  - Kalshi 2026-10-14: GA governor D 0.455; GA Senate D (Ossoff) 0.9515, vol 498,459; Lt. Gov D 0.495 (vol 1,830, **thin**). Insurance Commissioner D about 0.30 and School Superintendent D about 0.30 (zero volume, R incumbents).
  - *Estimated* P(D wins D3) ≈ 0.55 and P(D wins D5) ≈ 0.45. The two races are highly correlated, so P(both) ≈ **0.40** (range 0.30-0.55).
- **Policy.**
  - Hubbard's campaign "demanded an independent audit of Georgia Power's profit margin," which it says is above national averages, and wants data centers to pay their own costs ([peterforgeorgia.com](https://www.peterforgeorgia.com/), [Atlanta Press Collective](https://atlpresscollective.com/2026/10/13/georgia-public-service-commission-races-explained/)).
  - In May 2026, a Hubbard amendment for a fuel-cost and hedging review failed 3-2 on party lines ([Griffin Daily News](https://www.griffindailynews.com/news/state/public-service-commission-approves-georgia-power-storm-recovery-plan/article_8c02350a-555b-50a5-9e58-7658489b6f23.html)).
  - The Republican nominees defend the existing large-load rules (Johnson) or worry about overbuild (Tolbert) ([AJC](https://www.ajc.com/politics/2026/04/power-bills-data-centers-dominate-republican-georgia-psc-debate/)).
- **Enactment.** The rate freeze runs through 2028, so the lever is the next base rate case (file by July 2028; decision around December 2028; rates from 2029) ([10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/92122/000009212226000006/so-20251231.htm)). *Estimated* P(ROE set point cut by at least 50bp more than an R board would allow | D majority) = 0.70. Rationale: a 3-2 majority decides the case, and Georgia's 10.5% exceeds the national average. Offsets are a one-vote margin, settlement dynamics, and the credit-rating and capital-plan ($10B/yr) pressure.
- **Size.** The national average authorized electric ROE was 9.84% in 2025 (9.77% excluding riders) per RRA ([S&P Global, Sep 2026](https://www.spglobal.com/market-intelligence/en/news-insights/research/2026/09/average-gas-roe-edges-down-as-electric-holds-firm-in-h1-2026)). Georgia sits at 10.50%. A move to the national average is about 66-73bp. Range used: 50-75bp.
- **Exposure.** Georgia Power common equity is $27,034M (10-K, *verified*). It grows with $10B/yr of capex, so the 2029 figure will be larger (not included).
- **Dollars.** `$27,034M equity x 0.625% ROE cut = $169M net income / (1 - 0.252 combined federal+GA tax) = $226M/yr operating-income equivalent` (50bp → $181M; 75bp → $271M). Expected: `$226M x 0.40 x 0.70 = $63M`.

### Channel 2: Certification of further capacity (longer-dated)
- On 2025-12-19, the outgoing all-Republican PSC voted 5-0 to certify 9,885 MW (about $16B), about 90% of it for data centers in 2028-31. Staff had recommended about 3 GW. The PSC "has time to alter or reverse certification" if data-center contracts are not signed ([WRDW](https://www.wrdw.com/2025/12/19/state-regulators-ok-georgia-power-plan-serve-data-centers/), [WSB-TV](https://www.wsbtv.com/news/local/atlanta/psc-approves-16-billion-georgia-power-plan-despite-protests/WU4EYVDWSZGS7LLCDKBDMKNSJE/), [10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/92122/000009212226000006/so-20251231.htm)). Hubbard called that vote rushed ([Atlanta News First](https://www.atlantanewsfirst.com/2025/12/22/incoming-psc-member-skeptical-massive-energy-boost-is-ratepayers-best-interest/)).
- Market: KXGA4GW-26AUG-28MAR01 (≥4,000 MW more certified by 2028-03-01) is bid 0.62 / ask 0.65, vol 4,338 (**thin**).
- **Dollars (*estimated*).** `4 GW x ($16B / 9.885 GW = $1.62B/GW) = $6.5B rate base`. Then `x (0.56 x 10.5% / 0.748 + 0.44 x ~5% debt cost) ≈ x 0.100 = ~$660M/yr operating income at run-rate (2030+)`. *Estimated* P(blocked or deferred | D majority) = 0.25: Georgia Power backstops the costs, the load is contracted, and there is economic-development pressure. Expected: `$660M x 0.40 x 0.25 = $66M`. Reversing the already-certified 10 GW is treated as unlikely (contracts signed: 11 GW reviewed, plus 5 GW pending system-wide; 10-Q Q2 2026).

### Channel 3: Georgia governor (GOVPARTYGA-26)
- The PSC is independently elected. The governor's levers are filling PSC vacancies (Kemp appointed Wise in 2026), signing or vetoing energy legislation, and economic-development policy.
- Bottoms (D) would pursue a temporary statewide pause on new data-center construction. She is unsure whether that needs legislation. Jackson (R) wants local approval and cost conditions instead ([WABE](https://www.wabe.org/debate-over-data-centers-in-georgia-heats-up-governors-race/), [Atlanta News First](https://www.atlantanewsfirst.com/2026/09/14/bottoms-jackson-trade-hypocrisy-claims-bitter-clash-over-georgia-data-centers/)).
- Enactment *estimated* at 0.10: the General Assembly has had Republican majorities since 2005 (no market found) and an executive-order route is legally uncertain.
- **Dollars (*estimated*).** Data centers are driving Georgia Power's weather-adjusted commercial sales growth (+6.0% YTD 2026; 10-Q). `Pause cuts about 2%/yr of Georgia Power retail revenue growth x $11,110M retail revenue x ~60% non-fuel margin (estimated) ≈ $130M/yr`. Expected: `$130M x 0.455 x 0.10 = $6M`.

### Channel 4: Federal tax credits
- The OBBB (P.L. 119-21, signed 2025-07-04) restricted wind and solar 45Y/48E credits and added foreign-entity limits. Treasury interim guidance came out 2026-02-12 (10-Q Q2 2026).
- Southern Power's PTCs cut its tax expense by $33M in 2025, and its amortized ITCs were $58M (10-K/ARS).
- Changing this needs a law signed by the President. *Estimated* enactment 0.02.
- If it happens, +$0-50M (*estimated*: new Southern Power renewables only; regulated utilities pass credits to customers). Expected about $0.5M.

### Channel 5: Alabama
- The Alabama PSC associate seats (Places 1 and 2) are on the 2026 ballot. Place 1 is Matt Gentry (R) against James Gordon (D); Place 2 Republicans include Jim Zeigler (who beat the incumbent in the primary) against Sheila McNeil (D) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Alabama_Public_Service_Commission_election)). There is no market, and Republicans are heavily favored (the R governor nominee is at 0.9265).
- In any case, the Apr 2026 "Power to the People Act" bars base-rate increases before 2029-01-01, and the Dec 2025 PSC consent order holds rates through 2027 (10-Q). In-play value is about $0.

### Channel 6: FERC/EPA and grid-equipment tariffs
- EPA proposed repealing all power-plant GHG standards (June 2025) and repealed the 2009 vehicle endangerment finding (2026-02-12) (10-K). A Democratic Congress cannot reinstate these rules without the President, and there is no CRA route for reinstatement.
- Section 232 steel/aluminum/copper tariffs (10-50%) need a veto-proof law to override.
- Management estimated tariffs could raise base-capital-plan costs by 1-3%, with USMCA goods largely exempt. Mitigation comes through contingencies and regulatory recovery ([Q1 2025 call coverage](https://www.insidermonkey.com/blog/the-southern-company-nyseso-q1-2025-earnings-call-transcript-1523846/)). For a regulated utility, higher capex mostly enters rate base and is not an operating-income loss.
- Expected about $0.

## Locked in regardless
- Georgia Power's base rates are frozen for 2026-2028: ROE 10.50%, range 9.50-11.90%, earnings above 11.90% shared 40/40/20 ([SO 8-K](https://sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/41091/000009212225000066/so-20250701.htm), 10-K).
- Storm recovery: $869M over 67 months ($156M/yr) plus $31M/yr. Fuel billings cut by $394M/yr from 2026-06-01 (fuel is pass-through with no net-income effect). $77M of 45U credits went to customers (10-Q Q2 2026).
- The 9,885 MW certification of Dec 2025 (about $16B), which supports rate-base growth.
- Alabama base rates cannot rise before 2029 (state law).
- The OBBB credit restrictions, while the 45U existing-nuclear PTC and the Vogtle 3/4 advanced-nuclear PTCs continue.
- Section 232 metals tariffs. These are mostly a capex pass-through.

## Biggest uncertainties
1. **PSC race odds.** There is no market or poll, so the 0.40 is built from proxies (2025 landslide, 0.455 for governor, 0.95 for Ossoff) and could plausibly be anywhere from 0.30 to 0.55. A D5 runoff on Dec 1 adds variance.
2. **Behavior of a Democratic board in the 2028 case.** The 0.70 probability and the 50-75bp size are estimates. Rate cases often settle, and a 3-2 majority depends on one vote. The case outcome also falls after the 2028 PSC elections (other seats), although the decision would come before new members take office.
3. Channel 2 depends on the timing and cost of capacity beyond 2029 and has the weakest sourcing.
