# Walmart (WMT): 2026 election exposure
Base: FY2026 (ended 2026-01-31) net sales $706.4B, operating income $29.8B (EDGAR, 10-K filed 2026-03-13). Walmart U.S. net sales $483.0B, operating income $25.2B; U.S. grocery $285.5B (59% of Walmart U.S.). ~1.6M U.S. associates, average frontline wage >$18.50/h. "Less than one third of what we sell in the U.S. is imported" (10-K).
Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/104169/000010416926000055/wmt-20260131.htm

## Bottom line
- In play (expected): about -$28M/yr (0.1% of operating income); if everything happens: about -$126M
- Locked in regardless: SNAP cuts already law (SNAP benefits ~$95.7B FY2025 -> ~$84.7B annualized Mar 2026; Walmart's ~24% share = ~$2.6B/yr less SNAP spending at Walmart, mostly recouped via other payments per CFO); Section 301 tariffs (10-12.5%) with no congressional override route; Walmart's IEEPA refund was ~$2.9B.
- Card fact: The midterms put about 0.1% of Walmart's profit in play. The big hits (SNAP cuts, tariffs) already passed.
- Strongest R-vs-D contrast: Wisconsin governor ($20 minimum wage pledge by Crowley (D) vs. none by Tiffany (R)): about -$58M/yr if enacted.

### 1. SNAP funding cliff
`SNAP benefits $7.06B/month (USDA, Mar 2026) x Walmart share 24% (Numerator) x 0.5 month disrupted x 0.30 not recovered x gross margin 24.2% = -$61M`. Walmart CFO said there was "a dip" during the Nov 2025 pause and it rebounded (CNBC 2025-11-20). Disrupted fraction and unrecovered share are *estimated*.

### 2-4. Minimum wage (formula: associates x 0.92 hourly x share below floor x average raise x 1,700 h)
- WI: `33,718 x 0.92 x 0.55 x $2.00 x 1,700 = $58M` (associates and $19.30 average full-time wage from Walmart's Wisconsin location-facts page; share below $20 and average raise *estimated*).
- PA: `59,978 x 0.92 x 0.05 x $0.50 x 1,700 = $2.3M` (average $20.01).
- GA: `65,369 x 0.92 x 0.08 x $0.60 x 1,700 = $4.9M` (average $18.93).

## Locked in regardless
- SNAP provisions of P.L. 119-21: CBO -$186.65B over 10 years (via CRS R48552). Repeal requires the President's signature.
- Tariffs: Section 301 (no disapproval route) and 232; ending them needs a veto-proof law (CRS R48435).

## Biggest uncertainties
- Walmart's wage distribution by state (not disclosed).
- How much of a SNAP disruption shifts to other payment methods.
