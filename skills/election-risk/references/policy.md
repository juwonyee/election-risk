(from the analysis itself) or `estimated` (your derivation, with the arithmetic).

## Pin down the policy

Quote what it actually does: measure or bill text, the candidate's platform page, the commission docket.
Note the effective date and phase-in. Platforms are not bills: say which one you are sizing.

## Size it

- Prefer the official estimate (fiscal note, CBO or JCT score, legislative analyst). Add independent ones
  (budget labs, academic work) as a range.
- State the unit (dollars a year, a rate, a wage floor) and who bears it (which industries, which firms).
- If sources disagree, give the range and why.
- Carry over the numbers the company formula needs (e.g. the new wage floor and its schedule).

## Can it happen?

For a race, the chance a policy follows each winner is **not estimated here**: it is the policy book's `p`
(`erisk book find`), which comes from the research-based probability table in `$candidate-positions`. For a
direct market, the market price is the chance. Use this file only to size the policy.

## Locked in or in play

Separate what is already law, or cannot change after this election, from what the election decides.

| Candidates | Campaign websites, debate transcripts on public outlets, reputable news |

## Political context (time-sensitive)

Written 2026-10-15 for the 2026-11-03 US elections. Re-verify anything that may have moved before relying
on it.

### Federal
- The President (R) serves to January 2029: federal law changes need his signature or a two-thirds override.
  Peak Republican crossover on recent veto-relevant votes: about 5 senators and 6 House members.
- Chamber odds (Kalshi, mid-October 2026): House D about 90%, Senate D about 62%.
- P.L. 119-21 (the 2025 reconciliation law) set SNAP cuts, tax changes, Medicaid changes, clean-energy credit
  phase-downs and multi-year enforcement funding: treat as locked in through 2028.
- SNAP is funded through 2026-12-11 under a continuing resolution; Kalshi prices a shutdown on Dec 12.

### Tariffs
- IEEPA tariffs were struck down on 2026-02-20; the Section 122 10% surcharge expired on 2026-07-24.
- In force: Section 301 tariffs of 10-12.5% on 60 economies (USMCA goods exempt) since 2026-07-24; Brazil 25%
  (Section 301) since 2026-07-22; Section 232 sector tariffs (steel, aluminum, copper 10-50%; autos 15-25%;
  lumber and furniture; semiconductors 25%; pharma).
- Congress has no disapproval route for 301 or 232; reversing them needs a veto-proof law.

### State level
- Governor races, ballot measures, utility commission seats and city policies are where the coming election
  changes the most, and where many markets exist. The full lists: `erisk markets pull`.
